# Pricing strategy (hypotheses to test — not published prices)

**Principle:** don't charge for what isn't validated yet. The AI score is uncalibrated; what is scarce and costly is *human verification*. Price that, and treat the free baseline as acquisition.

1. **Pilot (now → calibration):** everything free. Recruit 3–5 design-partner organizations (Teams) at a discounted or free seat price in exchange for expert-labelled submissions and feedback. Expert labels are the asset that makes scoring defensible.
2. **Tiers after the pilot**
   - *Explorer* — free: baseline + a monthly practice cap (acquisition, share-able passport).
   - *Professional* — individual subscription (test €15–30/month) including a few verification credits; extra verifications sold as credits.
   - *Teams* — per seat per month with a minimum (test €10–20/seat, ≥10 seats), trainer studio, programs, aggregates.
   - *Enterprise* — annual contract: SSO/SCIM, EU data residency, custom exercises/rubrics, dedicated reviewers, SLA.
3. **Value metric:** seats for organizations; verified skills (reviews) for individuals. Avoid per-token pricing.
4. **Unit economics to measure first:** LLM cost per assessment (stored per assessment), reviewer minutes per verification (drives the credit price), review attach rate, baseline→paid conversion.
5. **Regions/currencies:** EUR first (FR/EU); consider a MENA price band and invoice/bank-transfer payment for B2B. Keep prices in one place when billing is built.
6. **Billing:** none implemented. First customers: manual invoicing; add Stripe only when ≥5 paying accounts.

All figures are starting hypotheses for willingness-to-pay interviews, not market facts.
